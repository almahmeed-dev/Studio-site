NextStep

Know your next step. Leave on time.

NextStep guides you through everyday routines on iPhone and Apple Watch, one clear step at a time. Keep your finish-by time in view while you get ready, pack your bag, or wind down.

Available on the App Store for iPhone and Apple Watch.

A routine that follows your lead

  • Create a routine with step durations, instructions and optional steps, or customize a starter.
  • Tap Done when you finish. Reaching zero shows overtime and keeps the same step active.
  • Pause, resume or add step time while your finish-by deadline stays fixed. Change the deadline only when you choose to.
  • Review a catch-up suggestion before skipping any optional future steps.
  • Run saved routines independently on Apple Watch. Paired devices exchange updates when a connection is available.
  • Type a task and review suggested steps before they join a routine. Steps come from NextStep’s own rules on your iPhone, and from Apple’s on-device model only for tasks they do not cover, where it is available.

Start with a short practice run

The 45-second demo has three steps. Try a pause and finish each step yourself. Practice stays out of your real history and duration suggestions.

Free and Pro

Free includes two saved routines, unlimited starts, routine editing, reminders, essential widgets, seven-day history viewing, local duration suggestions, data export/import and three tries of Break a task into steps. NextStep Pro adds more saved routines, full history views, advanced time comparisons, additional routine collections and unlimited Break a task into steps. For a first install of version 1.1 or later, Pro also includes Watch complications; people who used NextStep before keep them free.

Existing routines remain available to run and edit if Pro ends. Older history stays stored and exportable. Purchase screens never interrupt an active routine. Available monthly, annual or one-time lifetime offers and their current prices are shown by Apple before purchase.

Your routine stays yours

No NextStep account, advertising, third-party analytics or external AI service is required, and the task you type to break into steps stays on your iPhone. Routines and history are stored on your devices; paired sync and files you choose to share can transfer that content. Read the privacy policy for the details.

Alerts depend on notification permissions, Focus, device state and system delivery. NextStep is a planning aid, not a guarantee that you will finish or receive an alert at an exact time.

Questions people ask

What is NextStep?

A routine timer for iPhone and Apple Watch. It shows one step on screen with your finish-by time beside it, so getting ready, heading out or winding down becomes a short run of steps.

Does a step move on by itself when its time runs out?

No. When the countdown reaches zero, NextStep shows overtime and keeps the same step active until you tap Done. Done, Skip, Pause, Add 1 minute and End are always your call.

What is included without Pro?

Two saved routines that you can run as often as you like, routine editing, reminders, widgets, the Live Activity, the Start routine control, the last seven days of history, estimate suggestions, file export and import, three tries of Break a task into steps and a 45-second demo run.

What does NextStep Pro add, and how is it sold?

No two-routine limit, full history with planned-versus-actual time comparisons, extra ready-made programs, unlimited Break a task into steps, and Watch complications for people who first installed version 1.1 or later. Pro is sold as a monthly subscription, an annual subscription or a one-time lifetime unlock; Apple shows the price for your region before you confirm.

What happens to my routines if Pro ends?

Every routine you made stays available to view, edit and run. Older history stays stored and exportable. A paywall never interrupts a routine in progress.

Does the Apple Watch app need my iPhone nearby?

No. The Apple Watch app runs saved routines by itself, and the two catch up when they reconnect.

How does Break a task into steps work? Does it use AI?

Type a task and NextStep suggests steps and times. Common tasks use NextStep's own templates; on iPhones with Apple Intelligence, Apple's on-device model helps with the rest. Nothing is sent to an outside AI service, and you check every step before it is added.

Will a reminder start a routine automatically?

No. A reminder nudges you at the time you pick; you start the routine. Delivery depends on notification permissions, Focus and your device.

Can I use NextStep without opening the app?

The Home Screen widget and the Live Activity have a Done button for the current step. A Start routine control works from Control Center, the Lock Screen or the Action button, and Siri and Shortcuts can mark a step done, skip it or tell you what's next. Some of these need a recent iOS version or supported hardware.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no NextStep account. Routines and history stay on the devices that run them, and you can export a file whenever you want a copy.

NextStep next to other routine tools

Plenty of tools can time a morning. These are the usual ones and where NextStep differs.

The Clock app's timers and alarms
One countdown at a time. NextStep chains steps, keeps a finish-by time in view and waits for you to finish each step.
To-do and checklist apps
They track what to do, not how long each step takes in order. NextStep times each step and shows what a pause or extra minute does to your finish time.
Calendars
Good for blocks of time across the day. NextStep is for the minutes inside one routine.
Other visual schedule and routine apps
Many cover similar ground. NextStep's focus is a fixed deadline you can see, a standalone Apple Watch app, step times that learn from your own runs, and no account.
